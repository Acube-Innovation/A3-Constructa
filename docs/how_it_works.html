1. The problem this solves

On a construction job, money is committed long before it is spent, and spent long before anyone sees a number.

You order steel in March. It arrives in May. It is used in June. The invoice is paid in July. By the time the cost appears in the accounts, the decision that caused it is four months old. Meanwhile the site is asking for more material, the client wants a progress claim, and nobody can say with confidence whether the substructure package is still inside its budget.

A3 Constructa closes that gap. Every purchase order, delivery, material issue and invoice is tagged to a part of the works, so at any moment you can answer three questions:

QuestionWhere the answer comes from
What did we budget? The approved BOQ, allocated across the works
What have we committed? Purchase orders raised but not yet invoiced
What have we actually spent? Invoices posted and material issued from store

The difference between the second and third answer is the one most systems miss. A package can look healthy on spend and already be over-committed on orders that have not yet been invoiced. This system shows both side by side.

The three tags that make it work

Nearly everything in the system carries the same three tags. Once you understand these, the rest follows.

Cost Head The package of work
“University”, “Hospital”, “Road works”
▶
WBS The part of the works
“Substructure”, “Superstructure”
▶
Cost Code The kind of cost
Concrete, rebar, blockwork, plant hire
Every order, delivery, issue and invoice carries these three tags That is what lets the system add up cost by package, by part of the works, or by type of cost.
In practice: when a storeman issues 50 bags of cement, he picks the cost code. He does not need to know about accounts or cost centres — the system takes those from the cost code and posts them correctly.

Each of the other packages has its own breakdown beneath it, and each part of the works its own cost codes. The tree is as deep as the job needs — a part of the works can have sub-parts.

Why the cost code matters most: it is the level at which a quantity surveyor actually works. The BOQ says you allowed 4,000 bags of cement for the substructure. The cost code is what lets the system tell you that 1,450 have gone out and you are 36% through the allowance.

6. The construction-specific parts

Standard ERP handles buying, stock and accounts. These are the pieces that were added because a construction business needs them and off-the-shelf systems do not have them.

Imports tracked door to door

A single record follows a container from the supplier’s factory to the site store: FAT inspection, stuffing, sailing, arrival, customs, inland haulage. It holds the bill of lading, certificate of origin, duty receipt and delivery order, and records demurrage and detention when a container is held too long. Reports show turnaround by stage, so you can see whether the delays are at the port, at customs, or on the road.

Retention handled properly

When subcontractor work is certified, the system withholds the retention percentage and posts it to a separate Retention Payable account. The subcontractor is paid the net amount; the retention stays on the balance sheet until the defect liability period expires, at which point it is released deliberately rather than forgotten.

Small tools tracked by person

Tools are not treated as fixed assets — they are stock, issued into a custody store in an individual’s name. At any point you can list what each person is holding, how long they have had it, and what it is worth. Lost tools are written off with the amount recovered from the holder recorded against them.

Labour costed to the works

Attendance is recorded against a project and site, and timesheets against a WBS and cost code. Staff who fill timesheets have their hours costed directly; site labour who do not have their payroll spread across the projects they were present on, in proportion to days worked. Every figure says which basis it used, so nothing is silently half-measured.

Plant that earns or costs

Each machine is numbered by its own category — HEQ for heavy equipment, VEH for vehicles — and carries its make, model, capacity, serial and warranty. Breakdowns, repair costs and spare parts are recorded against it, and utilisation is measured against downtime, so the decision to keep a machine or hire one is based on its own numbers.

The demonstration system is loaded with one real-shaped project so you can follow a single cost from beginning to end. Here is the cement.

StageWhat happensResult
1. Allowed The BOQ allows 4,000 bags for the substructure Budget set against the Concrete cost code
2. Planned Needed on site in two weeks; supplier takes three The system says the order should already have been placed
3. Ordered 2,000 bags ordered from the cement works Money now shows as committed, not yet spent
4. Delivered Goods receipt into the central store Stock on hand rises; the order stops being a commitment
5. Moved 600 bags sent upriver by barge; 450 arrive so far 150 bags visible as stock in transit
6. Used 1,450 bags issued to the substructure Now a real cost against the Concrete cost code
7. Billed Supplier invoice received, due date passed Shows in payables ageing and the overdue figure
8. Reported Cost Code Wise Costing is opened Budget, committed, actual and balance for that one cost code

The same trail exists for the imported steel, the blockwork subcontract with its retention, the excavator and its breakdown, the tools in people’s custody, and the two progress claims to the client — one paid, one overdue.

Worth showing live: open Cost Code Wise Costing, then change the grouping from Cost Code to WBS to Package. It is the same money answered three ways, and it makes the point about the tags better than any explanation.

What the demonstration does not show

Two screens are deliberately empty: subcontract purchase orders, which need the standard subcontracting setup configured, and internal transfer despatch notes, which need an internal customer defined. Both are configuration decisions for the live system rather than missing functionality.
